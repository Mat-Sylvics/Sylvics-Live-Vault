# Knowledge

After this page you can save a source, write a literature note about it and turn one of its ideas into a permanent note.

> [!tip] The rule behind this page
> **The Authorship Test**: someone else's words go in Resources, their idea in your words in Literature, your own idea in Permanent. All ten rules: [[The rules of the vault]].

## Three steps

| Step | Folder | What the note is | Written in |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Source | `04 - Resources` | A record of something someone else made: a book, an article, a paper, a podcast, a video | Their words and facts about the work |
| 2. Literature note | `05 - Literature` | Your account of what one source says about one subject | Your words, about their idea |
| 3. Permanent note | `06 - Permanent` | One idea you now hold, stated as a sentence | Your words, your idea |

Most sources stop at step 1. A few deserve step 2. One or two ideas a month reach step 3. That is normal and it is the point: each step is a filter.

## A worked example from Mira's vault

Open these three notes in order. Each links to the next.

1. **The source.** [[🔬 How are habits formed - Modelling habit formation in the real world]] is a research paper. The header records the authors, the publisher, the date and Mira's reading status. Below it are a few quick jots, the paper's core thesis and the limits of the study. The note is about the paper.
2. **The literature note.** [[📖 Lally on how long a habit takes]] is her account of what the paper found, in her own words, under **Synthesis**, with the passages she marked under **Top Quotes & Ideas**. Its title follows a pattern: the author, then the subject. One source can produce several literature notes on different subjects.
3. **The permanent note.** [[💎 A habit takes months and not three weeks]] is the idea she took away. The title is a full statement you could agree or disagree with. The argument sits under **Core Argument** and **Why This Matters**, and it can be read without the paper. It links to the literature note it came from, to a neighbouring idea ([[💎 One missed day does not break a habit]]) and to a habit she changed because of it ([[🔹 Phone out of the bedroom]]).

That last link is where knowledge turns into action. A permanent note that changes nothing you do is still worth keeping. One that links to a habit or a project is the best kind.

## Step 1: save a source

`04 - Resources` has five sections. Open [[0. Resources Masterpage]] to see them.

| Section | Holds | Masterpage |
|---|---|---|
| Sources | Books, articles, research, podcasts, videos, films, courses, news, short clips, music | [[00. 📚 Sources Masterpage]] |
| Micro-knowledge | Quotes, facts, definitions, formulas, concepts, frameworks | [[00. 🧠 Micro-Knowledge Masterpage]] |
| Entities | People, companies, places | [[00. 🌐 Entities Masterpage]] |
| Reference library | Things you look up: technical, professional, personal, health, recreation | [[00. Reference Masterpage]] |
| Tools and assets | Software, code, design assets, AI prompts | [[00. 🛠️ Tools & Assets Masterpage]] |

Each type of source has its own masterpage, and they all work the same way:

| Part of the page | What it does |
|---|---|
| Four numbers at the top | Count the notes of that type. On the Books page: **Reading Now**, **Favorites**, **Average Rating**, **Total Logged**. |
| **➕ Log New ...** | Creates a note of that type from the right template, in the right folder, and opens it |
| Tabs | Sort the notes by status, each tab with its count. On the Books page: **Inbox**, **Reading**, **Favorites**, **Library**, **All**. |
| Search box and **Sort** menu | Narrow and order the tab you are on |
| Grid and list buttons | Switch between cards and a compact list. The page remembers your choice. |

Books, films, podcasts, videos, music, courses and short clips show their covers in the grid. A card shows the status, the rating and, where the type has it, the progress. Click a card to open the note. Click the heart to make it a favorite.

For a book, open [[📕 0. Books Masterpage]] and click **➕ Log New Book**. The note is created in the Books folder as `📕 New Book`. Rename it to the book's title and fill the header: author, status, rating. Under the numbers, the box **Reading Progress** lists the books you are reading with their pages.

The pages for facts, definitions, formulas, concepts, frameworks, companies and places are built the same way. One level up, [[00. 📚 Sources Masterpage]], [[00. 🧠 Micro-Knowledge Masterpage]] and [[00. 🌐 Entities Masterpage]] show one card per collection with its number of notes.

[[💬 0. Quotes Masterpage]] shows four numbers at the top (**Favorites**, **In Rotation**, **Unique Authors**, **Total Logged**), the quote today's daily note uses, and three tabs:

| Tab | Shows |
|---|---|
| **Library** | Every quote, with its author and the work it comes from, a search box and a sort menu |
| **Favorites** | The quotes whose heart you clicked |
| **Daily Rotation** | Every quote with a switch. The quotes you switch on can become the quote of the day on a daily note. See [[02 Your day]]. |

**➕ Log New Quote** asks for the quote, then who said it (pick one of your figures, or type a name) and which work it comes from (pick a source in your vault, or none). It creates the note in the Quotes folder, linked to both, and opens it. Leaving the first question empty cancels.

Do not try to save everything. Save a source when you expect to come back to it.

## Step 2: write a literature note

1. Open [[📖 0. Literature Masterpage]].
2. Click **➕ Create New Literature Note**.
3. Type a title in the form `Author on subject` and click **Create**. The `📖` is added for you.

The note opens in `05 - Literature`. Then:

- Write what the source says under **Synthesis**, in your own words, as if explaining it to a friend.
- Put the few sentences you want word for word under **Top Quotes & Ideas**.
- On the **Links** tab, add the source under **Knowledge & Resources**.
- Set **Status** to `🟡 Processing` while you work and `🟢 Processed` when it is done.

One literature note covers one subject from one source. A long book may give you four notes or none.

## Step 3: write a permanent note

1. Open [[💎 0. Permanent Masterpage]].
2. Click **💎 Create New Permanent Note**.
3. Type the idea as a full statement and click **Create**. The `💎` is added for you.

A good title makes a claim: "A habit needs a fixed cue more than it needs motivation", not "Habits". Then:

- Write the idea in a few sentences under **Core Argument**, so that it makes sense with no source open.
- Link the literature note or notes it came from, in the text or under **Knowledge & Resources**.
- Link at least one other permanent note. An idea with no links is hard to find again. The Permanent Masterpage lists such notes under **🚨 Orphan Catcher**.

The same page has a **🎲 Roll** button that shows a few of your permanent notes at random. Use it to look for links you missed.

## Topic maps

When a dozen permanent notes circle the same subject, a map note gathers them. Mira has six, in the folder `00 - Atlas/3. 🗺️ Maps of Content` and shown on [[00. Maps of Content Masterpage]]. [[03. 🏁 Finishing & Habits]] is the one that contains the example above. A map is an ordinary note with links and a few sentences on how the ideas relate. Write one when you notice a cluster. Do not plan them in advance.

## Watching the flow

[[0. Knowledge Pipeline]] shows how many notes sit at each stage: Inbox, ideas, resources, literature, permanent and maps. If resources pile up and literature stays empty, you are collecting and not reading. The weekly review shows the same picture for one week. See [[06 Reviews]].

## Naming

| Type | Starts with | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Book | `📕` | `📕 Thinking, Fast and Slow` |
| Research paper | `🔬` | `🔬 How are habits formed - Modelling habit formation in the real world` |
| Literature note | `📖` | `📖 Lally on how long a habit takes` |
| Permanent note | `💎` | `💎 A habit takes months and not three weeks` |

Some pages recognise a note's type by the emoji at the start of its name, and it makes search results easy to scan. Keep it when you rename a note.
